## Limits and expectations

“Erase my information from the internet” is not a universal one-click operation. Data can remain in source systems, public records, search caches, archived pages, screenshots and brokers that republish it. Rights vary by jurisdiction, and new sources may recreate listings. Do not assume any AI agent can independently prove every copy vanished.

## Privacy-first self-service process

1. **Define scope** for your own public information: name variants, public profiles, historical contact pages and data-broker directory entries you are legally entitled to control.
2. **Review visible public results** carefully, avoiding unnecessary uploads of personal documents to untrusted aggregators. Distinguish another person with the same name.
3. **Record minimal evidence** locally: provider name, listing URL, claimed personal data categories, verification method, date, applicable opt-out mechanism and status. Keep this private; don't create a shareable doxxing list.
4. **Use verified provider opt-out channels** or privacy-rights request forms. Do not send government ID, home address or sensitive identifying documents until necessary and after confirming a legitimate recipient and data-handling policy.
5. **Remove information at its source** where you control the account/site/profile, and review old public posts, domain WHOIS privacy and apps exposing contact details.
6. **Search-engine removals:** use relevant self-removal/outdated-content requests for eligible sensitive information or stale snippets; delisting from search does not erase source content.
7. **Track responses and deadlines** with retained receipts; follow up when requests are rejected or still visible.
8. **Recheck periodically** if personally useful; only submit removal requests for your own data or with documented permission.

## Rights and service constraints

- GDPR/UK GDPR and selected US state laws may provide access, correction, deletion or opt-out rights subject to exceptions. Public government records and journalism may have separate protections.
- Brokers may require identity confirmation; limit disclosure and use dedicated contact addresses where appropriate.
- Some listings are inaccurate matches, and opt-out to the wrong provider can expose more information than it removes.
- Automating bulk requests from unauthorized accounts or to unrelated people can be harmful or violate terms.
