## Practical reference extraction protocol

**Choose the right source for the design decision, not the most fashionable website.**

1. **Name the target task**: onboarding, dashboard scanning, content reading, checkout, brand film, homepage trust, etc.
2. **Select three references** matching that task and audience; record URL/date/viewports.
3. **Describe mechanisms in words**: grid, hierarchy, negative space, attention flow, transition purpose, interaction state and accessibility behavior.
4. **Mark transferable versus protected details**: pacing idea yes; one-to-one keyframes/brand motion no. Typeface role yes; proprietary font without license no.
5. **Build an original direction** using actual brand tokens and real content.
6. **Inspect the real rendered page** on phone, keyboard and desktop; profile motion/reduced-motion and readability.
7. **Judge whether the interaction actually improves the user's job**; novelty isn't UX evidence.

### Example: a minimal personal journal

Possible rule: “One strong page title and one meaningful recent-entry list; editorial whitespace separates themes; typography remains readable; no decorative hero animations.” This is **our interpretation of the design principle**, not a claim that SiteInspire publishes that specific app pattern.

### Example: a motion-rich product site

Define hero pacing as “brand promise appears first; demonstration second; supporting proof third; CTA available without waiting for scroll animation.” Use original assets, licensed fonts, keyboard-accessible controls and reduced-motion alternatives.
