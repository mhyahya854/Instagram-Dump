# Reel Notes 12 — App Launches, Marketing Routines, Pricing and Homepage Clarity

_Captured 2026-10-25. Advice and opinion from reels; do not treat anecdotes, income claims or platform statistics as validated.*

## Reel A: Four-step launch and marketing routine

The creator (“Goran” as spoken) says shipping apps is now easier than distribution. He promotes a routine allegedly associated with four apps and a company named “Automateeam,” with a self-reported >$500k in two years. **No independent financial verification supplied.** These are case-study claims, not guarantees.

### Step 1 — Be discoverable and publish consistently

- Identify relevant app directories, launch communities, industry indexes, review sites and product discovery platforms.
- Make accurate listings tailored to each site's rules. **Do not mass-submit spam, fabricate reviews or violate submission terms.** Automation can prefill data for review but should obtain approval for account creation, public posting and final submissions.
- Publish useful short-form demos or educational posts on platforms where the intended customers actually spend time (reel suggests Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts and X).
- The reel says “one video a day,” which is a *cadence example*, not a law. Authentic output and audience fit matter more than volume.

### Step 2 — Reduce the landing page to a clear proposition

The visitor should quickly understand what the product does, for whom, why it matters, and the primary next step. Reel suggests one clear promise and one call to action, but “no feature list ever” is **too absolute**: enterprise/technical buyers may need details, pricing, integrations, trust and policies. Use progressive disclosure rather than hiding important information.

### Step 3 — Time to first value

Aim to let a user experience a real result early, avoiding unnecessary sign-up/onboarding barriers. A “first minute” is a **hypothesis** depending on task complexity, not a universal cutoff. Regulated or high-risk apps may require consent/verification before providing a result.

### Step 4 — Earn attention rather than defaulting to ads

Organic distribution, demonstrations, partnerships, referrals and credible education can be effective. “One viral video beats months of paid ads” is an anecdote, not an experimentally verified strategy. Measure conversion quality and long-term retention instead of vanity reach.

## Reel B: A startup pricing skill

The reel claims a Claude skill can decide exactly what to charge and select optimal tiers. **Exact skill repository is unidentified**; do not invent a name.

A credible pricing analysis includes:
- Buyer, job-to-be-done, alternatives and switching cost.
- Measured/estimated willingness to pay (interviews and actual experiments).
- Variable costs per active user, API inference, storage, support, payment fees and fraud loss.
- Pricing metric (seat, organization, usage, unit outcome or flat subscription) that tracks customer value.
- Gross margin, churn/retention, acquisition cost, trial-to-paid conversion and geographic tax/legal constraints.
- Scenario modeling: conservative/base/optimistic; clear uncertainty and demand assumptions.
- Ethical tier structure with honest limits and no artificial bait pricing. **Do not force three pricing cards** because AI usually generates them.

### Illustrative unit economics (hypothetical)

If a $20/month plan has $5 in variable service/compute costs and $1 in payment fees, *contribution before fixed costs* = $14. If acquisition cost is $70, simple payback is 70/14 = **5 retained paid months**, assuming no other costs and no churn. This is a teaching model, **not a recommended price**.

## Reel C: Good versus bad homepage for a coach/service business

**Bad example:**
- Useless splash / welcome screen requires an extra click before seeing the actual service.
- Visitors hunt across sections to find offer, proof or booking information.
- Claims of trust appear too late; path to desired action unclear.

**Good example:**
- Above the fold: specific headline telling **what the business provides and who it serves**, relevant substantiation and a clear action (view services, book consultation).
- Content explains the visitor's problem, offered solution, approach, relevant pricing/service details and expected next steps.
- Display genuine social proof only when permission, identity and claims are verifiable, with context. No invented logos or numbers.
- Give service descriptions, case outcomes where documented, authentic contact methods and accessible interaction.

**Nuance:** Do not force every business homepage into one funnel or make a professional the “hero” at the expense of user's questions. Research the actual visitor intent.

## Launch measurements to consider

| Stage | Measurement | Misleading shortcut to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | Qualified organic or referral visits | Inflating traffic through bot hits |
| Understanding | Visitors who recognize product value, measured with usability tests | Assuming large headlines prove clarity |
| Action | Primary CTA click → completed booking/signup | Fake confirmation on failed forms |
| First value | Time to meaningful successful task | Rewarding superficial clicks |
| Retention | Real repeat use by relevant cohorts | Counting unattended background activity |
| Economics | Paid conversion, gross margin, support/inference cost | Assuming “viral” means profitable |
| Trust | Real outcomes, verified testimonials, refunds/complaints | Fake star ratings and social proof |
