## Reel B: skill packs do not substitute for taste

Normalized translation of the longer Hindi/English message:
> “You'll find many GitHub repositories and skills to help with editing. But if you don't know how the effect is made, you won't even know what to search for. You can show AI an editing reference, but it may miss *why* each element exists, so its output may be very different from your reference. Editors need visual identity and storytelling judgment, not just software skills.”

The phrase “editors don't need skills/software, they need taste” should **not** be read literally: skilled editors need both craft expertise **and** tools. The teaching point is that technical reproduction without editorial intent can yield visually plausible but incoherent results.

## What editors actually design

| Editorial question | Skill to learn | Concrete planning artifact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What should the viewer understand/feel? | Storytelling, narrative logic, pacing | One-sentence message, audience and emotional arc |
| Why is this scene present? | Narrative selection and scene purpose | Shot/beat sheet with intended takeaway per shot |
| What is the visual personality? | Art direction, visual identity and composition | Color/type/motion reference board with original design decisions |
| What makes motion readable? | Timing, easing, anticipation, rhythm and hierarchy | Timeline with keyframes, foreground-background priority |
| What sound supports the action? | Foley, sound design, music/speech hierarchy | Audio cue sheet with timing and rights |
| How do we know the result works? | Perceptual QC, accessibility and audience testing | Playback report, captions, audio normalization, visual continuity checks |

### A practical “reference-to-intent” worksheet

- Reference URL/file with permission to use.
- **Borrow:** legibility, pacing, framing, abstraction or scene transition *mechanism*.
- **Do not copy:** protected logos, brand identity, soundtrack, identifiable character design, unlicensed clips.
- Narration topic, audience, runtime, aspect ratio, content sensitivity.
- Color and type system, story arc, composition rules, audio hierarchy.
- Why each visual element exists, not only how it looks.
- Rendering stack, hardware constraints, accessibility (captions and photosensitivity).
- Evidence: shot-by-shot playback and correct final export.

## “Vox-style” reference caution

Vox's recognizably branded motion-graphic style can inspire high-level educational-explainer principles (clear diagrams, synchronized narration, sequential reveals). **Do not reproduce protected trademarks, full compositions, branded assets or unique footage without permission**. Develop an original system of color, shape, typography and sound rather than representing output as an official Vox production.

## Suggested offline learning exercises

1. **One claim, three different treatments:** illustrate the same fact as a clean chart, drawn stickman scene and labeled photo montage. Compare comprehension, not just “wow.”
2. **One effect, three motivations:** take a zoom/pan and explain when it reveals evidence, transitions scenes, or creates disorienting decoration.
3. **Audio-off test:** ensure key information remains comprehensible through captions/text/visuals.
4. **No-reference test:** reconstruct a complete short explainer from its own spec to check whether the document captures enough decisions.
